tddlog results

The release run of tddlog against the systems people use for each kind of problem: ProbLog, PITA and aspmc on logic programs, Ace, Merlin, pyAgrum, pgmpy, libDAI and TensorInference.jl on Bayesian and Markov networks, ProvSQL on probabilistic databases, and Dice, Alea.jl, Roulette, Geni, noDice, Psi, Genfer, SPPL, Pluck, Storm and PRISM on probabilistic programs. Each system ran single-threaded, in the same job and the same lanes of one machine as the systems it is compared with, and every answer of tddlog's is checked against a reference. Where another system is faster, the findings say so.

A plot counts the instances each system answers within a time limit, on a log scale from 0.2 s to the suite's limit: a line further up and to the left is better, and a run that takes less than 0.2 s counts at 0.2 s, since differences of a few milliseconds of start-up are not compared. A family's first plots are its kinds of problems, as in the README. A kind is one set of instances, the programs naturally written in the languages of that kind, and its systems are those whose language is of that kind, with ProbLog, PITA and aspmc on every kind: tddlog reads ProbLog, and they are given the program tddlog reads. The bit-blasted circuits are a kind of their own: Alea.jl compiles each of these programs, written in Alea.jl, to a circuit, and every other system is given that circuit, an intermediate representation rather than the program as written. Every system of a kind is tried on all of its instances, and an instance a system does not answer counts as unanswered, whatever the reason: out of time or memory, a wrong answer, a program the system cannot express, or one it was not given. The legend gives each system's answers and its total time over the instances that some system of the kind answers, a run that does not answer counted at its suite's time limit and a time under 0.2 s at 0.2 s; the README gives beside it the totals over the instances both tddlog and the other system answer. A suite's plot draws the systems that were given its instances. The findings compare tddlog with another system on the instances both answer: where each is faster, by how much, and their total times over those instances. An instance that both finish in under 0.1 s counts neither as a win nor as a loss. The numbers come from the same records as the README's and docs/benchmarks.md's.
