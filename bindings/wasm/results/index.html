tddlog results

The release run of tddlog against the systems people use for each kind of problem: ProbLog, PITA and aspmc on logic programs, Ace, Merlin, pyAgrum, pgmpy, libDAI and TensorInference.jl on Bayesian and Markov networks, ProvSQL on probabilistic databases, and Dice, Alea.jl, Roulette, Geni, noDice, Psi, Genfer, SPPL, Pluck, Storm and PRISM on probabilistic programs. Each system ran single-threaded, in the same job and the same lanes of one machine as the systems it is compared with, and every answer of tddlog's is checked against a reference. Where another system is faster, the findings say so.

A plot counts the instances each system answers within a time limit, on a log scale from 0.2 s to the suite's limit: a line further up and to the left is better, and a run that takes less than 0.2 s counts at 0.2 s, since differences of a few milliseconds of start-up are not compared. A plot draws every system over all of its instances, an instance a system does not attempt counted as unanswered, so that it shows coverage rather than speed. Its legend gives how many instances each system answers, and how many are its own (from its own papers, benchmarks or distribution), natural to it (written as its users write programs, in a translation that stays close to the original and does none of its work for it: no grounding, unrolling or bit-blasting) and attempted (given to it at all, natural or through such a translation). Under the plot of a family's problems, a small plot for each cluster of systems covers the instances natural to all of them, with tddlog and every system they are all natural to. The README's speed comparisons are over each system's natural instances, the total times over those either answers and over those both answer; under the plot of every problem, what each system is compared on and why. The findings compare tddlog with another system on the instances both answer: where each is faster, by how much, and their total times over those instances. An instance that both finish in under 0.1 s counts neither as a win nor as a loss. The README compares by total time instead, a run that does not finish (out of time or memory, failed, or skipped after a smaller instance of its family failed) counting as the time limit. The numbers come from the same records as the README's and docs/benchmarks.md's.
